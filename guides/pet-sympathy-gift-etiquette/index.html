Pet Sympathy Gift Etiquette

By the Again Paws studio · October 2026

Giving a gift when someone loses a pet is increasingly normal — but the rules are unwritten, and getting them wrong makes an awkward moment worse. This guide covers who should give, when, how much to spend, and the situations people actually worry about.

Is a pet sympathy gift appropriate at all?

Yes — for anyone you'd send flowers to after a human loss. Surveys consistently show a majority of grieving owners felt their pet's death was not taken seriously by people around them; a gift that acknowledges the loss stands out precisely because most people send nothing. The only real mistake is a joke gift ("happy the cat is gone" mugs have ended friendships).

Timing: when to send it

  • Within the first week: a card or message. This is the "I see your loss" moment.
  • 2–6 weeks after: the best window for a gift. Support floods in during week one, then evaporates — right when the quiet house starts hurting.
  • The first anniversary or holiday: a small token is deeply appreciated and almost nobody does it.

How much to spend, by relationship

  • Coworker: a card, $15–30 (flowers, donation, small keepsake).
  • Friend: $30–100. A wind chime, engraved stone or framed photo.
  • Close friend or family: $80–200+. A handmade portrait of their actual pet is the heavyweight option — see what's available at each budget.

Tricky situations

  • You never met the pet. Fine — gifts made from photos don't require you to have known the animal. Order from photos your friend already posted.
  • The pet is dying, not dead. Wait. A gift arrives better after the loss; a message of support can go now.
  • Religious or cultural concerns. Photo-based keepsakes are safe across beliefs; skip anything with afterlife imagery unless you know their views.
  • A group gift. Pooling money for one quality keepsake beats five cheap items — one person collects and orders, everyone signs the card.

Frequently asked

Is it weird to give a pet memorial gift to a coworker?

A card with a personal line about their pet is perfect for most offices. If the team was close to the pet (office dog), a group gift is common and appreciated.

How long does a custom keepsake take to arrive?

Handmade pieces typically need 2–4 weeks plus shipping — which is exactly why they land in the quiet weeks after the loss, when support has dried up. Our timeline guide explains the process.

Should the gift include a message?

Always — and mention the pet by name. The card is often kept longer than everything else.
