What to Say When Someone's Pet Dies

By the Again Paws studio · October 2026

When a friend loses a pet, most people freeze — is it "just a dog"? It is not, and the research is clear that pet grief is real grief. The right words are simple, specific and free of clichés. Here are twenty messages you can send today, plus what to avoid and when to send something more than words.

Short messages for a text or card

  • "I'm so sorry about [name]. He was such a good boy, and he was so loved."
  • "[Name] was lucky to have you. Thinking of you both."
  • "No one could have asked for a better cat mom. I'm here if you want to talk or sit quietly."
  • "I remember when you first brought her home. What a lucky life she had."
  • "Sending love. Losing a friend like that leaves a real hole."
  • "[Name] had the best life a dog could have. That's because of you."

Messages that share a memory

The most treasured messages mention the specific animal — an anecdote only you know:

  • "I still laugh about the time [name] stole an entire sandwich off the counter. He was a legend."
  • "She always sat on my feet like she owned them. I'm going to miss her."
  • "That dog greeted me like a returning soldier every single visit. I loved him too."

What NOT to say

  • "It's just a cat/dog" — never, under any circumstances.
  • "You can get another one." A pet is not a subscription.
  • "At least he didn't suffer" / "At least she was old." Minimizing reads as dismissive.
  • "I know exactly how you feel." You don't; make it about them, not you.

Frequently asked

How soon should I reach out after a pet dies?

Within a day or two — pet grief is sharpest in the first week and most people receive nothing. A late message ("I just heard about [name]") is still welcome weeks later.

Should I mention the pet by name?

Yes, always. Grieving owners consistently say hearing the pet's name matters more than any phrasing — it confirms the animal mattered to someone else too.

Is a memorial gift appropriate if I didn't know the pet well?
