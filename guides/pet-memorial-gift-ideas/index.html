10 Pet Memorial Gift Ideas

By the Again Paws studio · October 2026

When someone loses a dog or a cat, ordinary sympathy gifts don't quite fit. The best pet memorial gifts do one thing: they turn a memory you can't hold into something you can. Here are ten ideas, from handmade keepsakes to living tributes, with honest notes on cost and timing.

1. A custom needle-felted portrait from a photo

A small wool sculpture of the actual pet — same markings, same tilted ear — is the closest thing to holding them again. Made from photos, it works even when the only pictures are old phone snapshots. See how our memorial keepsakes are made; prices start at $87 for a mini portrait.

2. A framed photo with a custom engraved mat

Simple and fast (days, not weeks). Print the best photo, add a mat engraved with the pet's name and dates. Cost: $30–60 at any print shop.

3. A paw-print cast or ink print kit

Ink kits ($15–25) work if the pet has passed but prints were taken; clay castings need to be made while the pet is still alive or from an existing print. If no print exists, a felted portrait from photos is the alternative.

4. A memorial stone or garden marker

Engraved stone for a garden or burial spot. $40–100, weatherproof, zero upkeep.

5. A living tribute plant or tree

A hardy indoor plant or a tree planted in the pet's name. Growth becomes an ongoing, quiet memorial. $25–150.

6. A donation in the pet's name

To a rescue or the vet school that treated them. Costs whatever you choose, and for many grieving owners it is the most meaningful gesture of all.

7. Custom jewelry with fur or ashes

Glass pendants or resin rings containing a lock of fur. $60–200. Beautiful, but order early — most makers need 4–8 weeks.

8. A portrait painted from photos

Watercolor or oil portraits from photos, $80–500 depending on the artist. Ask to see photos of finished work before paying a deposit.

9. A star or flower naming

Novelty registries will "name a star" ($30–60). Sentimental value varies — know your person before choosing this one.

10. A handmade memorial ornament

Felted or ceramic ornaments ($25–90) are especially appreciated around the first holiday without the pet — the hardest one of all.

How to choose

Ask one question: does this person want to see their pet every day, or hold something when it hurts? Wall pieces serve the first; handheld keepsakes like our felted portraits serve the second. Whatever you choose, include a handwritten note mentioning the pet by name — it costs nothing and matters more than the gift itself.
