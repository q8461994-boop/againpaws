12 Cat Memorial Ideas

By the Again Paws studio · November 2026

Losing a cat leaves an absence shaped exactly like them — the 6am wake-up call that stops coming, the hallway that nobody supervises anymore. Cats are creatures of specific places and rituals, and the best memorials honor that. Here are twelve ideas, from window tributes to keepsakes made from photos, at every budget.

Window and indoor memorials

  • A portrait suncatcher on their window. Cats live in sunbeams — a portrait suncatcher on the window they claimed throws their colors across the room every morning, right where they used to sit.
  • A needle-felted portrait of your actual cat. A handmade wool sculpture matching every marking — the crooked whisker, the white toe. Works from any clear photos, even for cats who have passed. Our memorial keepsakes start at $87.
  • Keep their spot, marked. A small framed photo or plaque on the shelf, chair or cat tree they owned. Free if you own a frame — their collar draped over the corner does a lot of quiet work.
  • A custom wind chime near their favorite window. A photo wind chime turns every breeze into a small hello.

Garden and outdoor memorials

  • A memorial garden stone with a photo. For the porch, the flowerbed or the sunny patch they patrolled — weather-safe garden stones with a ceramic photo window ($79+) survive every season.
  • Plant catnip or cat grass over their resting place. Practical and symbolic — something growing that they would have destroyed within minutes, and loved every second of.
  • A dedicated flower pot. A planter they never bothered (a miracle, in hindsight) with a small marker tucked at the base.

For their ashes

  • A custom cremation urn with their portrait. A hand-painted urn in four colors turns storage into tribute.
  • A farewell remembrance set. For families who follow the paper-offering tradition, a complete farewell set honors the passing properly.
  • Scatter in a meaningful place. The garden they watched birds from, or a quiet corner of the yard — check local rules for shared spaces.

Still deciding what to do with the ashes themselves? Our guide to what to do with pet ashes covers nine options, including the legal and practical details.

Living tributes

  • Donate to a shelter or rescue in their name. Especially powerful if yours was a rescue — other cats get the second chance you gave yours.
  • Keep a whisker. Cats shed whiskers; many owners find one saved in a drawer years later. A tiny vial or locket turns an accident of shedding into a keepsake.
  • Print their best 50 photos. $20–40 for an album of the sunbeam naps and box sits — best made together as a family.

Choosing between them

One question sorts it: do you want a place to visit, or something to hold? Garden stones and planted catnip serve the first; handheld keepsakes like felted portraits serve the second. Many families end up with one of each — a stone by the flowerbed, a portrait on the shelf. Frequently asked

What is a good memorial for a cat?

Something tied to the places your cat actually loved — a suncatcher on their window, a garden stone by their sunny patch, or a felted portrait from their photos. Cats own specific spots in a home; marking one feels far more personal than a generic urn.

How much should a cat memorial cost?

Meaningful options exist at every budget: a framed photo with their collar is nearly free, window suncatchers and garden stones run $40–100, and handmade felted portraits $87–477 depending on size.

Can a memorial be made if I only have old or blurry photos?

Usually yes — experienced makers work from phone photos all the time. Send what you have and ask honestly what's possible before paying anything.

Is it strange to hold a funeral for a cat?

Not at all. A small farewell — a few words, a burial or cremation, a marker — helps children and adults alike process the loss. The scale is yours; the ritual is what matters.
